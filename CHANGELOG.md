# Changelog

Each release of the `chessdrive` Plugin bumps `version` in `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` and adds a line here, newest first. People who added the marketplace get it from Check for updates, or `claude plugin marketplace update` and `claude plugin update` in Claude Code. A zip doesn't update, and uploading over it changes nothing: people remove the Plugin, download it again and upload it.

- **0.25.1** (2026-11-06): `chessdrive-patterns` says 30 games take about 5 minutes on every Plan: Free now checks six games at a time, as Hobby does, so a run on Free no longer takes 8.
- **0.25.0** (2026-11-05): `chessdrive-patterns` groups your mistakes by what they allowed as well as by the board. The chess model writes one line on each mistake from the engine's lines, eight a call, Embed takes the lines and the positions, and the groups come from both, so a name fits its examples more often: on two players' games, two names in three held, against one in three before. Each example's explanation says how it shows its group's name where the engine's line does. The Skill quotes about 45 cents and 5 minutes, or 8 on Free, as two runs measured; the chess model's lines are about 3 cents of that, and under a minute.
- **0.23.1** (2026-11-05): `chessdrive-game-review` says who stood where in the engine's own words. Its script prints "about equal", "a little better", "clearly better" or "winning" for the position before and after each move, from the evaluation's band, and the answer copies them, so a move that threw away a win is no longer called a draw thrown away. A video or article gets its Study too, a heading's mark is its verdict's, no inaccuracy joins the list while a mistake or blunder of yours is there, a reply the chess model cut short by its own thinking is asked for once more, and a reply's claim of a piece won or a named pattern stays only when the engine's Line shows it.
- **0.23.0** (2026-11-05): The game fetcher downloads only the games you want. `fetch_games.py` takes `--speed`, one or more of bullet, blitz, rapid, classical and correspondence, and `--since` and `--until` dates: Lichess sends only those games, and chess.com reads only the months in range and keeps their games of that time control. `--how-long` counts the games of that scope. When a player has over 500 games and you didn't say which, your agent asks which time controls and how far back before it downloads, so "my rapid games" no longer waits for every bullet game.
- **0.22.0** (2026-11-04): One position costs nothing. A Study's "Engine's line" runs Stockfish in your browser, free, with no key, and its questions still use the page's Study key. Where code runs, `chessdrive engine` analyses one position below depth 20 on the Stockfish installed on your machine, free, and says how to install it if there's none. ChessDrive's Engine, now $4.50 an hour of Engine time, does whole games, lists of positions, several games at once and searches at depth 20 or more. `chessdrive-patterns` quotes about 35 cents. The free local engine needs the next CLI release; an older CLI runs every position on ChessDrive's Engine.
- **0.21.0** (2026-11-04): The Skills call ChessDrive through the `chessdrive` CLI wherever your agent runs code, Claude's chat app included, and use the Connector only in a chat that runs none, or if you'd rather not install the CLI. Game review gets a Lichess or chess.com game from its link on your machine. `chessdrive-patterns` marks your mistakes with `chessdrive engine --games` and a short example your agent adapts, so "count my inaccuracies too" counts them. No Skill names a Plan's limits any more: the CLI reads them from ChessDrive. Needs CLI 0.6.0.
- **0.20.0** (2026-11-04): `chessdrive-patterns` keeps a group's name only when at least three of the five examples checked show it, and says how many groups it left out. Each group it keeps shows three examples from different games, each with a line from the chess model on what the move allowed, from the engine's lines, and a script builds the Study in the frame, so the run is quicker: about 4 minutes, or 5 on Free.
- **0.19.0** (2026-11-04): A Study answers about the position you're on. Its "Ask about this position" card shows the engine's evaluation and best Line, and answers one question about the position with that Line, from Queen, or Knight on Free past Queen's limit. It runs only when you ask. A Study built by a coding agent comes with its own Study key, which calls only the engine and the chess models, up to $1 a day, for 7 days, and is listed on the Dashboard under Study keys. A downloaded copy carries no key: it shows a quiet "Add your key" line, and a key added there stays in that browser. In Claude's chat app the page can't reach ChessDrive, so a Study there works as before. Needs CLI 0.6.0 for `chessdrive study-key`, or the Connector's `study_key` tool.
- **0.18.0** (2026-11-04): Every Study looks like ChessDrive and has the same layout: a header that says what it is and how to use it, the Board, and a panel beside it, or under it on a phone or in a narrow artifact. The Board and its buttons stay in view on any screen. In a coding agent the Study opens by itself and its path comes as a link, and Download saves a copy that opens with no internet, or Save as PDF. Agents no longer test the page in a browser.
- **0.17.0** (2026-11-04): One sign-in. In Claude Code the Connector uses the key `chessdrive login` saved, so it no longer asks you to sign in again through `/mcp`; it needs CLI 0.5.0, and without the CLI it signs in through the browser as before. The set-up steps the Skills give show Codex and Cursor how to use the same key.
- **0.16.2** (2026-11-03): The Skills read the Connector's leaner `engine` answer, whose moves are SAN alone: `chessdrive-position` draws its best move on the board by its SAN, and a Study gets an arrow's squares from the Board itself.
- **0.16.1** (2026-11-03): `chessdrive-prep` says who played what. The move after one of the opponent's lines, or a first move when they have Black, is often their opponents' choice, and the report no longer says the opponent played it. The plan's call to the chess model sends its message the way the tool takes it the first time.
- **0.16.0** (2026-11-03): Annotate's talking points read as a video script: each is a bold line to say on camera, then two or three sentences on what's going on, why it matters and what to point at, from the chess model's replies and the engine's lines, then the Line to show. A point with nothing to draw on gets one more call to the chess model, at most five in a run.
- **0.15.0** (2026-11-03): Ask about a position after any Skill, such as "what does the knight on f7 threaten?", and the answer comes from the engine and the chess model, never from the agent's own reading of the board, so it names no piece, square or threat the tools didn't. Every Skill that shows a position answers these as `chessdrive-position` does, reusing an engine answer it already has and asking the engine for only as much as the question needs.
- **0.14.1** (2026-11-02): `chessdrive-game-review` judges moves in words too, so a game with no mistakes no longer quotes how much win chance a move cost or how many pawns the evaluation moved.
- **0.14.0** (2026-11-02): Skills run their calls side by side. `chessdrive-patterns` sends as many games to the engine at once as your Plan allows, so checking 30 games takes about half as long on Hobby, with more to gain on Pro. Free still checks one at a time. Game review, Annotate and Patterns ask the chess model two at a time. The cost of a run doesn't change.
- **0.13.0** (2026-11-01): The Skills carry their own instructions, so a Use case's Copy prompt is a line or two, and each Skill's steps are at `/skills/<name>.md` for an agent with only the Connector. Prep ends in a Study in Claude's chat app too, as Game review does. `chessdrive-vectors` says what's true: it runs in Claude's chat app on a paid plan. Where the sandbox can't reach Lichess or chess.com, a Skill gives you the link, on words, and never opens a browser to get around it. The Skills were tightened.
- **0.12.3** (2026-11-01): `chessdrive-patterns` quotes about 25 cents, as Queen's prices fell 40%.
- **0.12.2** (2026-11-01): A Study's Board moves to `@chessdrive/board` 0.1.2: the squares stay put when your own move replaces every verdict on a game, a line plays at a third of the speed, so you can follow it, and a Game review shows its evaluation from the first position.
- **0.12.1** (2026-11-01): A Study's Board moves to `@chessdrive/board` 0.1.1, whose squares stay put when the evaluation goes, as when you try your own moves.
- **0.12.0** (2026-11-01): Boards take moves. `chessdrive-position`'s first board does, so it no longer offers to play the position out. In a Study, a Board that shows a game or a position takes your moves too, and "Back to the game" returns to it.
- **0.11.0** (2026-11-01): `chessdrive-prep`: prepare for one opponent from their username or a PGN file of their games. Your agent fetches their public Lichess and chess.com games on your machine, a script counts what they play against your colour, and the engine checks where their top lines end. You get their form, a game plan, and what the games leave out, on a board. `chessdrive-study` can now build an Opponent prep page.
- **0.10.0** (2026-11-01): The Skills get you going in your own agent. Give a username and your agent fetches the games itself, on your machine. Where it can't, as in Claude's chat app, or for ChessBase, the Skills give the exact steps to export a file. When the CLI, the sign-in, the Connector or Claude's domain step is missing, they say which and walk you through it, one step at a time. Ask what a Skill does and it says what it needs in a line or two.
- **0.9.1** (2026-11-01): In Claude's chat app the CLI comes from npm, and you ask for it by name in your message, as Claude's safety check allows that and nothing else.
- **0.9.0** (2026-11-01): `chessdrive-patterns` and `chessdrive-game-search` run in Claude's chat app on a paid plan, once you've added our domain under Settings, Capabilities. The agent brings the CLI into Claude's sandbox and signs in once per chat, and what it writes there resets with each new chat.
- **0.8.1** (2026-11-01): The Plugin is on a public repository, `edgecaseai/chessdrive-plugin`, as a marketplace, under the MIT license. The README installs from there.
- **0.8.0** (2026-11-01): The Skills show boards in the answer, on `show_board` or in a Study, and link a move only where neither works. Answers read as one list: the answer in bold, then a headline and a sentence or two for each item. The cost comes up only if you ask, or before a run over $1.
- **0.7.4** (2026-11-01): The Skills check many positions in one Engine call instead of one call each, so there's no burst of calls. `chessdrive-game-search` checks up to about 500 positions a question with `chessdrive engine --positions`, which needs CLI 0.3.0, and never runs an engine on your machine.
- **0.7.3** (2026-11-01): The Skills leave `max_tokens` out, so a reply isn't cut short, and carry on without a note when Generate can't answer.
- **0.7.2** (2026-11-01): `chessdrive-patterns`, `chessdrive-game-search` and `chessdrive-vectors` check the CLI's version first, and ask you to update an old one instead of failing partway.

- **0.7.0** (2026-10-31): `chessdrive-game-review` opens a Lichess or chess.com link with the Connector's new `get_game` tool, so a pasted link works in Claude Chat. A chess.com link asks for your username once.
